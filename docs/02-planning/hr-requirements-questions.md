# HR Assist — Requirements Discovery Questions (HR Team Call)

> **Meeting:** Requirements call with the HR team
> **Date:** 2026-10-05
> **Roles:** HR team = **product owner** · Employees = **users**

**Goal of the call:** find out what problem HR wants to solve, who will use the assistant, what it should and shouldn't do, and what "done" looks like, before any technical work is scoped. The questions are written in plain, non-technical language.

---

## 1. Purpose & Problem Statement
*Why this is being built.*

1. What's the main problem you're hoping this solves for employees today?
2. Is something specific driving this now? For example, HR gets too many repetitive questions, employees can't find policies easily, or responses take too long.
3. What would success look like for your team six months after launch?

## 2. Who Will Use It
*Pinning down the audience, since "employees" can mean different groups.*

1. Is this meant for all employees, or for one group first (e.g. one department, one location, or new hires only)?
2. Will it be used only by full-time employees, or also by contractors and interns?
3. Are several countries or regions involved, and do the policies differ by location?
4. Will managers need anything different from other employees (e.g. approving requests, not just asking questions)?

## 3. What Employees Will Actually Ask
*The core section: get concrete examples, not just categories.*

1. Can you walk us through the 5–10 questions HR is asked most often?
2. Are there topics where employees often get confused or ask the same thing more than once?
3. Are there busy seasons we should plan for (e.g. open enrolment, year-end leave questions)?
4. Are there questions HR can't answer quickly today that you'd like this to help with?

## 4. What Information Should Be Covered
*The content, in HR's own terms.*

1. Which policy areas should it cover at launch: leave, holidays, WFH, benefits, payroll, onboarding, anything else?
2. Where does the official version of this information live today (intranet, handbook, shared drive, SuccessFactors)?
3. Are there policies that vary by role, length of service or location, where the assistant must give the specific answer rather than a generic one?
4. Is anything currently undocumented or inconsistent that we should flag before using it as a source?

## 5. What It Should NOT Do or Say
*Limits matter as much as features.*

1. Are there topics it should avoid completely or pass to a person (e.g. grievances, terminations, legal matters, performance issues)?
2. Is there anything sensitive it should never answer automatically, even if the information exists somewhere?
3. When it isn't sure of an answer, should it make its best attempt, say it doesn't know, or always pass the question to HR?

## 6. Personal / Employee-Specific Information
*This decides how deeply it needs to connect to HR systems.*

1. Should it answer only general policy questions, or also personal ones like "how many leave days do I have left?" or "when did I join?"
2. If personal data is involved, who in HR can see that information today, and should the same rules apply here?
3. Is there any personal data (e.g. salary, medical leave details) you'd want kept out of this entirely, even if it's technically possible?
4. Is there a **test version of SuccessFactors** (often called a "preview" or "sandbox" instance, separate from the live system) that we could use to build and try out the assistant safely before it connects to real employee records? If so, who can arrange access for us?

## 7. Actions, Not Just Answers
*Should it only give information, or also do things?*

1. Should the assistant **only answer questions** (e.g. "how many leave days do I have left?"), or should employees also be able to **carry out actions** through it, such as applying for leave?
2. If actions are wanted, which ones matter most: applying for leave, cancelling or withdrawing a leave request, checking the status of a request, claiming a reimbursement, raising an HR ticket, something else?
3. Which actions should be available **at launch**, and which can come **later**?
4. When something creates a real request, who should be notified, and how (email, a ticketing system, something else)?
5. Should any of these requests need a manager's or HR's approval before they're final?

## 8. Escalation to a Real Person
*The human safety net.*

1. When it can't answer something, who should the question go to: a specific inbox, a team or a person?
2. How quickly should HR respond to forwarded questions? Is there a response-time target today?
3. Should employees be told that their question was forwarded, or should that happen in the background?

## 9. Tone, Voice & Identity
*Small, but it shapes many design decisions.*

1. Should it feel like a formal HR representative, or a friendly, casual assistant?
2. Should it have a name or persona, or simply be called "HR Assistant"?
3. Does HR already have tone guidelines for employee communications that this should follow?

## 10. Ownership & Maintenance Going Forward
*Who keeps it correct after launch? This is often overlooked.*

1. Once it's live, who is responsible for keeping the policy information up to date?
2. How often do these policies change, and how will we find out when they do?
3. Who should review and approve what the assistant says before launch: HR only, or also legal/compliance?

## 11. Rollout & Scale Expectations
*Realistic scope for a pilot versus a full launch.*

1. Would you like to start with a small pilot group, or launch to everyone at once?
2. Roughly how many employees do you expect to use it regularly?
3. Is there a target launch date, or does it depend on scope?

## 12. What Success Looks Like Long-Term
*Where this could go next.*

1. Beyond answering questions, is there a longer-term vision, such as this eventually replacing some manual HR processes?
2. Might other teams (IT, Finance) want something similar later? That could affect how we design it now.
